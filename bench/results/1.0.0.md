## Memory: the heap of a world

A new world takes 54,533 bytes instead of 52,004 (+2,529 bytes, averaged over 200 worlds kept alive):
the closures and small tables of the new features and of the fixes (toggles, creation tracking,
the bookkeeping of removal hooks and monitors, the floors of generations). It is a constant per
world, not a cost per entity, pair or query. The heap rows of P8 measure whole worlds at a
resolution of 1 KiB, so the constant flags every one of them whose noise is one step: 19 rows +2 KiB, 22 rows +3 KiB.
The bytes per distinct pair of the small scale divide the heap by the pairs of a small world, so
the constant shows there too
(native small +1.724 bytes);
the bytes per holder are unchanged. The heap after the lifecycle rounds and after the long
sessions grows by the same constant as the built world: nothing accumulates.
