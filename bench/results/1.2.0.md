## Memory: the heap of a world

A new world takes 56,586 bytes instead of 55,997 (+589 bytes, averaged over 200 worlds kept alive):
the closures of the world that raise errors for the mistakes of their callers keep the function
that raises them (`fail`) as an upvalue, and the one that takes the ticks of the change filters
keeps the function that checks their ids (30 bytes). It is a constant per world, not a cost per entity,
pair or query: the table of the world keeps its fields, and a pair record, a plain record and
an entity take the bytes they took in 1.1.0, so the bytes per distinct pair and per holder of
P8 are unchanged. A debug world, which the suite does not use, takes 60,216 bytes instead of 56,474 (the checks of its methods).
The heap rows of P8 take the heap before and after the world in whole KiB, each rounded down;
the heap before the world holds the code of the module, which differs, so its fraction of a KiB
differs between the builds, and a constant of about half a KiB moves a row by 0, 1 or 2 KiB. A
row whose noise is one step and that moved by two is flagged: interpreter client, client heap after 9000 more frames (+2 KiB); interpreter target-empty-retained, the heap after 3 000 more frames (+2 KiB).

The rows of bytes per distinct pair take the heap of the distinct pairs of a scale in whole KiB
too, divided by their number, so they move by steps: one KiB over the 594 distinct pairs of
the small scale is 1.72 bytes. One step up is flagged (interpreter small, +1.72 bytes against a noise of 1.02), and one step down is listed among the improvements (interpreter small-empty-retained, -1.72 bytes; native small-empty-retained, -1.72 bytes); a pair record keeps its size, and the target scale takes 1527.808 bytes per distinct pair in both versions.

Measured to the byte, with 64 worlds of the client profile built and run side by side (the
same in both modes): a client world takes 560 bytes more than in 1.1.0 after it is built and
after 3 000 frames, and 592 after 6 000 and after 9 000 frames; 1.1.0 and 1.2.0 grow alike
between 6 000 and 9 000 frames (by 0 bytes), so nothing accumulates.
