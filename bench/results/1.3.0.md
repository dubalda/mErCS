## Memory: the heap of a world

A new world takes 56,586 bytes in both versions (averaged over 200 worlds kept alive),
and a debug world, which the suite does not use, 60,216 in both.
1.3.0 adds no field and no closure to a world: the dispatcher of the listeners of a change is made
when the first of them connects, as in 1.2.0, and the function that reads a replaced value for the
rare paths of a pair is a function of the module. A pair record, a plain record and an entity take the
bytes they took in 1.2.0, so the bytes per distinct pair and per holder of P8 are unchanged.
The heap rows of P8 take the heap before and after the world in whole KiB, each rounded down;
the heap before the world holds the code of the module, which differs, so its fraction of a KiB
differs between the builds, and a constant of about half a KiB moves a row by 0, 1 or 2 KiB. A
row whose noise is one step and that moved by two is flagged: none.

The rows of bytes per distinct pair take the heap of the distinct pairs of a scale in whole KiB
too, divided by their number, so they move by steps: one KiB over the 594 distinct pairs of
the small scale is 1.72 bytes. One step up is flagged (native small, +1.72 bytes against a noise of 1.02), and one step down is listed among the improvements (interpreter small, -1.72 bytes); a pair record keeps its size, and the target scale takes 1527.808 bytes per distinct pair in both versions.

Measured to the byte, with 64 worlds of the client profile built and run side by side: a client
world takes the bytes of 1.2.0, within the 16–32 bytes by which repeated measurements of one build
differ: 130 400 after it is built, 182 464 after 3 000 frames (182 448 in one of the runs of 1.3.0 in
native code, 130 432 after the build in another), 182 592 after 6 000 and after 9 000 frames in both
versions and modes; it grows by 0 bytes between 6 000 and 9 000 frames, so nothing accumulates.
